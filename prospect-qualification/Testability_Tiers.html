Rules of thumb for reps

  • Ask for weekly online orders first4,500 a week is the minimum to engage; 6,500 is the target. Below 4,500, do not offer a trial.
  • High AOV rarely qualifies on purchasesFurniture, watches and jewelry: pitch micro-conversion tests (CTA clicks, appointments, leads) instead, as with Ethan Allen.
  • Template tests need twice the volumeA test on product pages only reaches about half of buyers, so it needs roughly double the orders.
  • Borderline means noWithin about 25% of the minimum? Treat it as below until the prospect shares real analytics.

How this was measured

Every PostHog project in the Fastr Workspace organization was scanned for the 30 days to 4 October 2026. Visitors are unique people with a real page view, bots excluded. Conversion rate is converting visitors divided by visitors, and orders are de-duplicated on transaction id, matching the Experiment Feasibility by Client workbook. Unique visitors grow more slowly than time, so reach is modelled as V(t) ∝ t0.92, the median measured across customers. Test size uses the two-proportion z-test (see Evan Miller's sample-size calculator).

PostHog counts only what Fastr tracks, so some customers' total orders may be higher. Four sites record purchases without visitor tracking (Thirty Three Threads brands, Kane's Furniture) and are not tiered yet. Full detail is in Fastr_Prospect_Testability_Scorer.xlsx.
